What is changing

Real estate assembled over a lifetime is passing to heirs who often have little experience owning or managing buildings, at a moment when the advisers closest to a sale are paid to lease or sell.

Rate increases since 2022, maturing debt, and shifts in office demand have broken prices in parts of the market.

Under current law, depreciation, cost segregation, and 1031 exchanges let direct owners of real estate defer tax and compound wealth across generations.

What it means for families

Heirs need a partner who can explain what they own, what to keep, and what to exchange, so the knowledge behind each asset passes along with the asset.

Forced sellers and overcorrected valuations create entry points for patient capital, and REALM’s families can act while lenders and institutional buyers wait.

Those benefits depend on a structure built around the family’s tax position.
