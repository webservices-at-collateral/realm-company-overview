What Family Capital Means

The four parts of the definition, and what each one asks of an investment manager.

Values

What the family stands for

The principles a family holds and expects of anyone who invests on the family’s behalf. Values decide which assets a family will own, which partners a family will work with, and what passes to the next generation.

Knowledge

What the family has learned

The judgment a family has earned building a business, owning property, and investing over decades. Shared with peer families and taught to heirs, that knowledge compounds alongside the family’s capital.

Resources

What the family has built

The capital itself, built through an operating business, real estate, or investing, and held with an eye to tax and to the generations that follow. Resources give a family the freedom to invest on its own timeline.

Relationships

Whom the family knows

The operators, brokers, advisers, and peer families a family has gathered over generations. Those relationships bring opportunities to the family before a marketed sale.
