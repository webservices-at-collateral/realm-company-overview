 Collateral Partners · Brand · Competitive Benchmark
 The field converges on a serif headline over a dark photograph, and REALM can stand where nobody does: a quiet screen built from its own mark 
 The field is framed on two axes REALM named itself: professional versus private capital against level of sophistication (t7 34:54 GD), and Rockefeller timelessness against Anduril’s modern edge (t6 11:17 GD). REALM’s target is the corner Greg drew as empty: high sophistication for private capital, in a timeless register with one bold, owned device. The short read: the category looks like a dark, serif, photographic first screen with a figure on it, usually behind a consent banner on a phone. No family-capital peer has an identity system; the one firm that uses arcs as a graphic is the cautionary case. 
 T1 direct · T2 substitutes · T3 other routes · T4 niche · T6 ceiling one company per row first screens captured at phone width, Oct 27, 2026 for the REALM brand 
 Executive summary · 30-second read
 01 The category default is dark, serif and photographic. Across T1 to T4 the phone first screen is a serif display line over black, navy, green or a building photograph, with one button. Figures appear on the first screen of the rows that sell to advisers. (02 line-up) 
 02 The strongest identities own one move and repeat it. Rockefeller Capital Management opens on a single monogram; Anduril on black and one photograph; YPO on blue block capitals. None needs its name read to be known. (T2/S02, T6/S04, T3/S02) 
 03 The field gets the first minute wrong. On a phone, consent dialogs or offering gates cover the first screen on many rows. A referred family’s first sight of the firm is a legal prompt. (02 line-up) 
 04 REALM owns something no peer has: a mark whose meaning (strategies working together, people drawn closer) can become the whole graphic language, and a reporting habit of candour that peers replace with figures (t7 44:50 TK; audit/O06). 
 05 The unclaimed position: a light, quiet first screen where the mark’s geometry is the image, one sentence about family capital, no figure, a person to reach. The only arc user in the field (Cadre) is the brand Travis named as the one that did not work (t2 31:04 TK), so the arcs must arrive in REALM’s register, not Cadre’s.

 00 Framing axes where REALM is, where it wants to be 
 Axis 1 · Capital served × sophistication
 Greg’s map: professional versus private capital on one axis, product sophistication on the other, with a giant hole here where sophisticated real estate meets private capital (t7 34:54 GD). Today: REALM’s surfaces sit low and generic: a collective with counters (audit/F04). Target: top of the private-capital side. Wealth platforms (Cresset, Rockefeller) sit high but are not real estate; pooled vehicles (BREIT, Fundrise) sit on the private side but low on choice.
 Axis 2 · Timeless × modern edge
 Greg: We're closer to the Rockefeller than to Anduril (t6 11:17 GD). Travis: Is this real? (t8 44:31 TK) and not vanilla (t8 56:18 TK). Target: timeless structure carrying one bold, owned device, the rule in the Master Creative Brief (agency).

 02 The line-up test the introduction check · phone first screens side by side 
 Run on the decisive surface (the introduction check): every captured row’s first screen at 390px wide, set beside REALM’s. The deck records the read; it does not reproduce the screens. The capture is in the project working folder for the designer.
 The default. A serif display line over black, navy, deep green or a dark building photograph, one button, and figures for the firms that sell to advisers (T1/S01, T1/S06, T2/S05, T3/S01, T4/S02, T6/S02; figures on T1/S03, T2/S07).
 The first-minute problem. On a phone, consent dialogs cover much of the first screen on many rows (T1/S01, T2/S01, T2/S02, T3/S01, T3/S03, T3/S05, T3/S06, T4/S01), and one row opens on a legal gate (T2/S04). A family who has just been introduced meets a prompt before a promise.
 Recognisable at a glance. Rockefeller (one monogram on green, T2/S02), Anduril (black and one photograph, T6/S04), YPO (a blue block, T3/S02) and Cadre (bright arcs, T2/S09). Each owns one move; none relies on its name.
 Where REALM disappears. A white ground, a boxed logo, a centred blue headline carrying the retired tagline and a counter (T1/S00). At phone width the arcs are too small to register, and nothing says family capital.
 The unclaimed space. A light, quiet first screen where the mark’s own geometry is the image, one sentence, no figure and a way to reach a principal, with privacy handled without a wall. Only Crosstimbers (T4/S03) is near it in tone and only Cadre (T2/S09) uses arcs; nobody does both, and Cadre does arcs in the register REALM rejects.
 02b Palettes in the field sampled by area · REALM first 
 Up to five colours per row, ordered by area, sampled from each row’s desktop first screen on Oct 27, 2026 (one from a phone capture, as labelled). The most saturated small colour is added as an accent where area sampling would miss it. First screens include photography, consent banners and overlays; each card says so. Treat these as reads of what a visitor sees, not brand specifications. REALM appears three ways: the colours its site declares, the colours its documents use, and what its home page shows.
 The read. The field sits in two families. Most rows are navy, black or deep green with white (Declaration, Blue Owl, SREIT, YPO, Sixth Street, Emergence, Thoma Bravo, Cadre, Rockefeller), and the peer networks and substitutes that warm up do it with photography browns and one accent: copper, orange, coral, teal or mint (10 East, Cresset, Long Angle, Origin, Cadre). REALM’s own colours are three navies and a mid-blue plus a neon yellow-green token, so it sits inside the first family and owns nothing. The unclaimed area is warm stone and charcoal on a light ground: only Crosstimbers (T4/S03) and Fundrise (retail) use a warm off-white, and nobody pairs it with charcoal. Proposed palette P2 sits there and is built from the logo’s own two tones; P3 sits nearest Rockefeller’s green (T2/S02).

 Where the market and the client disagree
 The market repeats whatever REALM last told it, so it disagrees with itself: collective, platform, multifamily office. The press also inflates scale (CNBC’s $12B under management), which is the opposite of Greg’s caution that trying to claim scale too soon is dangerous (t8 47:30 GD), yet close to Travis’s wish that at scale should feel like it's already been done (t8 25:52 TK). REALM wants to be known for family capital; the market files it beside proptech and luxury brokers with the same name. The brand’s first job is to give the market one sentence to repeat.

 What happens after the pick
 One mono study file per picked candidate (format grid, mark placement and scale, lockups, type classes, image role, small-size behaviour, fixed versus variable), with no reference artwork redrawn. Patterns go into the registry with anatomy; routes are composed from registry patterns, named for their final idea, shown at full, review and smallest size, mono first, with colour variants as separate files. Route CSS is scoped under .rt so it cannot collide with the deck.
